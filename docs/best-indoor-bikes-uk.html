Best smart bikes

Zwift Ride V2 with Wahoo Kickr Core 2

Best value smart bike
£1,099.99 RRP · often discounted to £899–£990
  • 1,800W max
  • 16% max gradient
  • ±2% power
  • Cranks 160–175mm
  • Riders 152–198cm

Zwift's smart frame sits on a Kickr Core 2 trainer, which does the measuring and resistance. The V2, launched in September 2026, added adjustable cranks, more saddle setback and a smaller frame at the same price. It is by far the cheapest way to get a smart bike, and since Zwift bought Rouvy the controls also work in Rouvy.

Pros

  • Half the price of most smart bikes
  • Built-in Zwift controls and virtual shifting
  • Light and easy to move
  • The trainer can be used with a normal bike later

Cons

  • Controls designed for Zwift; other apps need workarounds
  • Button feel is not as good as dedicated shifters
  • Max resistance and gradient lower than premium bikes

How to choose

  • Compatibility first: axle standard, freehub (Shimano HG, SRAM XDR, Campagnolo) and whether you want a cassette or Zwift Cog.
  • App: Zwift Cog and Click virtual shifting works fully in Zwift and now in Rouvy; check others before buying.
  • Accuracy: ±2% is fine for most people. Repeatability matters more than the headline number.
  • Noise: all direct-drive trainers are quieter than wheel-on ones; your chain and fan will be the loudest parts.
  • Budget for extras: a fan, mat and sweat cover add £80–£200. See accessories.
