The best indoor cycling apps

Your app matters as much as your trainer: it decides whether indoor riding feels like a game, a real road or a training lab. Here's what each one does well and what it costs in the UK.

The apps reviewed

Zwift

Best overall
£17.99/month

The biggest community, the busiest group rides and the best-organised racing. Virtual worlds are game-like, and structured workouts and plans are built in. Zwift bought Rouvy in April 2026, but both apps still run separately with separate subscriptions.

Pros

  • Always someone to ride with
  • Best racing scene
  • Works with Zwift Ride and Cog and Click

Cons

  • Price has risen over the years
  • Training analysis less deep than TrainerRoad
Visit Zwift

Rouvy

Best real-world routes
Around £17.99/month · group plans available

Ride filmed real-world roads with augmented-reality riders on screen, from Alpine climbs to Ironman courses. Rouvy now also owns FulGaz's route videos and Bkool. A group account lets up to five riders share one subscription, and Zwift hardware now works in Rouvy.

Pros

  • Real roads and famous climbs
  • Group account for families
  • Good for previewing races

Cons

  • Fewer riders around you than Zwift
  • Video quality depends on your connection
Visit Rouvy

MyWhoosh

Best free app
Free

Virtual worlds, workouts, training plans and races with no subscription. It hosts the UCI Cycling Esports World Championships through 2026. Graphics are good; the community is smaller than Zwift's.

Pros

  • Completely free
  • Proper training plans
  • Prize races

Cons

  • Quieter roads
  • Some users report bugs after updates
Visit MyWhoosh

TrainerRoad

Best for getting faster
Around £/$20/month

No virtual world: just power graphs and very well designed training plans that adapt to how your sessions go. Popular with time triallists and racers who want results rather than entertainment.

Pros

  • Best adaptive training plans
  • Plans work outdoors too
  • Excellent analysis

Cons

  • Little entertainment
  • Among the priciest apps
Visit TrainerRoad

Wahoo SYSTM

Best all-round training
Check current price

Wahoo's training app, built from The Sufferfest, combines structured workouts with yoga, strength and mental training, and uses a four-number fitness profile rather than just FTP.

Pros

  • Strength and yoga included
  • Detailed fitness test
  • Entertaining video workouts

Cons

  • No open-world riding
  • Smaller community
Visit Wahoo SYSTM

TrainingPeaks Virtual

Best if you use TrainingPeaks
Included with TrainingPeaks Premium

The former IndieVelo, now part of TrainingPeaks. A clean virtual world with racing and group rides, included in a Premium plan rather than sold separately.

Pros

  • No extra cost for Premium users
  • Coach-friendly
  • Improving quickly

Cons

  • Fewer routes
  • Rough edges
Visit TrainingPeaks Virtual

Other apps worth knowing

  • FulGaz: high-definition real-world routes, now owned by Rouvy.
  • Kinomap: large library of user-filmed videos, also for rowing and running.
  • Wattbike Hub: free with Wattbike bikes, with hundreds of workouts.
  • TrainerDay: a free or low-cost workout library and builder.

Every app here syncs your rides to Strava. Most offer a free trial: ride two apps for a week each before you commit.

Subscription prices change often and vary by plan; check each app's site. Sources include Cyclingnews and Bicycle Retailer.
